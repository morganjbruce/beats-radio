# Musical taste — feeling first, technique second

Music is not about notes — it's about what the listener feels in their body. Decide what the
room feels like, then choose the tools that inevitably create that feeling.

## Musical intuition
Imagine music as a room you place a listener inside. They don't hear "notes" or "patterns" —
they feel tension or ease, movement or stillness, intimacy or distance, warmth or cold,
confidence or fragility.

Bad music is usually not "wrong notes" — it's unclear intention. Every piece must answer:
**"how should the listener feel, physically and emotionally, over time?"**

## Mood → intention → mechanics
Each mood below maps an emotional posture (how the body feels) to a musical intention (what the
music is trying to do) and the concrete choices that create it.

**CALM / SAFE / GROUNDED** — relaxed shoulders, steady breathing; nothing urgent.
- Slow-to-moderate tempo (60–90 cpm, or slower like `setcps(0.7)` for atmospheric); stable harmony (avoid rapid changes); few voices; soft attacks (triangle/sine/filtered saw); low dynamics (gain 0.25–0.4); minimal randomness; `perlin` modulation for organic movement; detuned layers for width. Calm does NOT mean boring or sparse — it comes from tempo, harmony, and dynamics. If it feels jittery you failed; if it lacks harmonic foundation you also failed.

**GROOVE / CONFIDENCE / HEAD-NOD** — upright spine, steady nod; reliability + subtle swagger.
- Consistent grid; strong kick placement; repetition with micro-variation (not pattern changes); bass and drums tightly locked; avoid too many simultaneous ideas; swing/syncopation sparingly. Groove dies when you add "one more cool thing."

**TENSION / ANTICIPATION** — leaning forward; something is coming.
- Repetition without resolution; rising filter or density; limited pitch range that wants to escape; delay feedback slowly increasing; harmony that avoids the root. Do not resolve too early — tension requires restraint.

**RELEASE / EUPHORIA** — chest opening; relief and expansion.
- Harmonic resolution (arriving home); widened stereo; fuller spectrum (bass + highs); longer notes; fewer rhythmic interruptions. If everything is loud all the time, release feels meaningless.

**MELANCHOLY / INTROSPECTION** — inward gaze; reflection, not sadness-for-show.
- Minor modes or modal ambiguity; slower harmonic rhythm; simple melodies; gentle detuning or instability; silence is allowed. Do not dramatize — let it breathe.

**CHAOS / AGGRESSION (use rarely)** — adrenaline; overwhelm.
- Dense rhythm; distortion/harsh timbres; irregular accents; faster tempo. This is a spice, not a base — most good music avoids chaos and uses it intentionally.

## Time arcs, not loops
Music is not "a cool 1-bar idea repeated forever" — it's a listener being carried somewhere.
- Start simple; introduce one idea at a time.
- Change one dimension at once (rhythm OR harmony OR texture).
- Remove elements as often as you add them. Subtraction is a sign of taste.

## Anti-patterns — what kills taste
- ✗ adding randomness everywhere
- ✗ changing tempo or key without emotional reason
- ✗ adding more layers when something feels weak
- ✗ mistaking density for intensity
- ✗ writing "busy" code to seem impressive

Taste is restraint plus intention.

## Balance constraints — enforce these
- Palette of 5–6 sounds across the song; **3–5 voices sounding at once**, with the full stack saved for one brief peak. Maximum 2 layers per instrument type (avoids mud).
- Drums support, don't dominate; bass supports, doesn't dominate; effects subtle and supportive; no single element overwhelms.
- Typical voice structure: (1) bass — foundation, simple; (2) chords/pad — harmonic bed; (3) drums — groove, background; (4) optional texture, arp, or extra harmonic layer.

## Know your intent
A song works when you can answer:
1. What should the listener feel in their body?
2. What should change over time?
3. What should NOT change?

If unsure, get clearer and more intentional — not necessarily simpler, and never blander. A
distinctive idea executed cleanly beats both cluttered complexity and safe cliché.
